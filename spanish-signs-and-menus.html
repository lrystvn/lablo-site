Spanish signs and menu words for your trip

When you travel, the Spanish you need most isn't in conversations. It's on doors, signs and menus.

These are the words you'll actually run into, grouped so they're easy to scan. Where Mexico and Spain differ, both are listed.

Learn these on your own things. Point Lablo at your next sign or menu and every word gets labeled, with audio.

Mexico vs. Spain: the words that change

If you're learning for a trip, pick the version people use where you're going. In Lablo you can choose Spanish (Mexico) or Spanish (Spain) and it uses the right words automatically.

Use them in a sentence

  • La cuenta, por favor.
    The check, please.
  • ¿Dónde están los baños?
    Where are the restrooms?
  • ¿Tienen una mesa para dos?
    Do you have a table for two?
  • ¿Esto es picante?
    Is this spicy?
  • ¿Está incluida la propina?
    Is the tip included?

"Entradas" can trip you up: on a menu in Mexico it means starters, but on a sign it means entrances.

Questions

How do you say exit in Spanish?

"Salida." You'll also see "Salida de emergencia" for an emergency exit.

What does "jale" mean on a door?

"Jale" means pull, and it's used in Mexico. In Spain the door will usually say "Tire" or "Tirar." "Empuje" means push.

Can I translate signs with my phone?

Yes. Lablo's Text mode reads the sign or menu in your photo and translates it, so you can learn the words while you figure out where you're going.
